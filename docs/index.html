Abstract

The initialization problem in indirect optimal control

Indirect methods for spacecraft trajectory optimization offer superior solution accuracy and mass optimality, but require a precise initial guess for the costate variables — a notoriously difficult problem that causes standard solvers to fail on challenging transfer windows. We train a denoising diffusion model on a dataset of optimal Earth–Mars trajectories, then use it to generate costate initializations for a boundary-value problem (BVP) solver. Across 17 time shifts spanning one synodic period (~780 days) and 250 trials each, our method achieves 100% convergence on 13 of 17 windows, compared to 18–62% for the ε-continuation baseline — while producing identical final masses on all cases where both methods converge.

Method

Two-stage costate initialization

Stage 01

Diffusion Denoising

A transformer-based diffusion model, trained on 8,000 optimal trajectories, denoises a random noise trajectory conditioned on the departure and arrival boundary states. The 30-step reverse process produces a physically plausible state–costate trajectory (r, v, m, λr, λv, λm) at 32 collocation nodes.

Stage 02

BVP Refinement

The diffusion output initializes an IPOPT-based collocation solver (160 iterations, 8-point RK4 continuity) that enforces the Pontryagin optimality conditions exactly. Convergence is declared at continuity residual < 10−8; clean cases reach 10−15.
